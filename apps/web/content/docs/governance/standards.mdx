---
title: The standards behind the Constitution
description: SINA's rules aren't arbitrary numbers. Every governed threshold traces to a real regulation or an explicit, tunable policy — and the citation travels with the decision.
---

A governance system is only trustworthy if its rules are *defensible*. So in SINA,
every rule carries its source. When the gate blocks something, the violation
includes a `standard` — the regulation or policy it comes from — and that citation
lands in the audit record. A block is never a mystery.

There are two kinds of threshold, and the docs (and the code) always keep them
apart.

## Regulatory — grounded in law

These come from real financial regulations. They're not yours to loosen casually.

| Rule | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Currency Transaction Report (CTR) | $10,000 | FinCEN — 31 CFR 1010.311 |
| Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) review | $5,000 | FinCEN — 31 CFR Ch. X |
| Travel Rule (originator/beneficiary info) | $3,000 | FinCEN — 31 CFR 1010.410(e) |
| Crypto travel rule | $1,000 | FATF Recommendation 16 |
| Same Day ACH ceiling | $1,000,000 | Nacha |
| EFT disclosures | — | Regulation E |
| Credit / lending disclosures | — | Regulation Z (TILA) |

And the format rules — the ones that check an identifier is even real:

- **ISO 4217** — valid currency codes, and amounts counted in integer minor units.
- **ISO 13616** — IBAN structure (mod-97 checksum).
- **ISO 9362** — BIC / SWIFT codes.
- **ABA** — US routing numbers.
- **Luhn** — card-number checksum.
- **PCI-DSS-aligned handling** — card security codes and PINs are never stored; card
  numbers are masked to the last four.

## SINA policy — sensible, tunable defaults

These are product defaults, not law. They're a reasonable starting point, and they
are the ones you're expected to tune to your own risk appetite.

| Rule | Default | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Wire secondary approval (dual-control) | $50,000 | Four-eyes on large wires |
| Per-rail authorization caps | $2,500–$25,000 | P2P, bill-pay, ACH, FX, withdrawal |
| B2B maker-checker approval | $10,000 | Two-person sign-off |

<Callout variant="note" title="Which is which, always">
  Every threshold in the [fintech reference](/docs/reference/fintech) is labelled
  regulatory or policy. When you tune a number, you're changing a policy default —
  the regulatory ones you change at your own risk.
</Callout>
