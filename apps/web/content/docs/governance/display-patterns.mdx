---
title: Display patterns
description: Not all UI is an action. Showing a balance or a transaction list is a read — governed for shape and privacy, but never escalated.
---

A real app spends most of its time *showing* things, not *doing* things. Balances,
transaction lists, holdings, statements. SINA calls these **display patterns**, and
they're the ungoverned half of the system.

## Governed for shape, not for policy

A display rule has no limits and no approvals — there's nothing to authorize when
you're reading. But it still runs through the gate, and it still checks three
things:

- **Shape.** Strict parsing, same as everywhere. A fabricated row or an extra field
  is rejected.
- **Bounds.** Lists are capped, so a hostile stream can't send 10,000 rows to
  freeze the browser.
- **Privacy.** Sensitive identifiers must be masked. A display schema won't accept a
  full account or card number — only the masked form.

Pass all three and SINA mounts a presentational component — `TransactionList`,
`BalanceCard`, `PortfolioHoldings`, and their siblings. There are two dozen of
them, one per kind of read.

## The data comes from you, not the model

This is the quiet safety property. For a read, the model picks *what* to show
("list transactions"); your server supplies the actual values. So the model can't
invent a balance or hallucinate a transaction — it never touches the numbers.

## Reads are read-only

A display component never renders a raw action button. If the user wants to *do*
something from a list — dispute a charge, pay a bill — that becomes a **new intent**
that re-enters the gate as a governed action. There's no shortcut from "showing" to
"doing" that skips the check.

<Callout variant="note" title="Why this matters">
  It means the same guarantee covers your whole app. Reads can't leak or fabricate
  data; actions can't bypass governance. Every screen — boring or high-stakes —
  passed the gate.
</Callout>
