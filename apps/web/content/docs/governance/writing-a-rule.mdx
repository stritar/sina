title: Writing a rule
description: Every SINA rule answers the same eight questions. Fill them in and you have a governed intent — here's the template and the wire rule as a worked example.
---

A rule is just a schema plus some policy. But a *good* rule — one that's clear,
testable, and defensible — answers the same eight questions every time. Use this as
your template; the [Add a schema guide](/docs/guides/add-a-schema) has the code.

## The template

- **What it is** — one plain sentence.
- **Why it exists** — the risk it removes.
- **What it blocks** — a concrete bad request.
- **What passes** — a concrete good request.
- **What happens on trigger** — refuse, or escalate to which component?
- **Severity** — `flag`, `escalate`, or `reject`.
- **How to author it** — the shape (fields, types) and the policy check.
- **How to observe it** — what shows up in the audit record.

## The wire rule, filled in

- **What it is** — governs money moved by wire.
- **Why it exists** — large or under-documented wires carry regulatory and fraud
  risk.
- **What it blocks** — a $60,000 transfer with no second approver; a smuggled
  confirm button; a self-approval.
- **What passes** — a $500 transfer; or a $60,000 one with a valid second approval
  bound to the exact terms.
- **What happens on trigger** — over the limit → escalate to `SecureWireDialog`;
  fabricated fields or self-approval → reject.
- **Severity** — over-limit is `escalate`; reporting thresholds are `flag`; strict
  and four-eyes failures are `reject`.
- **How to author it** — a strict payload (amount in minor units, valid currency,
  IBAN/BIC or routing/account) plus a policy that checks the cited thresholds.
- **How to observe it** — each violation records its code, message, and cited
  `standard`; the audit event names the decided component.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Cite your thresholds">
  Give every number a named constant with its source beside it — like SINA's
  `thresholds.ts`. It makes the rule reviewable, and it distinguishes a legal
  requirement from a product default you can tune.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="note" title="Test it like an attacker">
  A rule isn't done when it compiles — it's done when a hostile request can't get
  past it. Pair every rule with adversarial fixtures. See [Write an adversarial
  test](/docs/guides/write-an-adversarial-test).
</Callout>
