---
title: Why machine-readable governance matters
description: An AI can read your style guide and ignore it. It cannot ignore a rule that runs as code. That difference is the whole point of SINA.
---

AI agents can now assemble interfaces on their own. That changes what a design
system needs to be.

## Reading the rules vs. obeying them

You can hand an AI your design guidelines as a document. It will read them — and
then, often enough, do something else. Not out of malice; it just pattern-matches
to what *looks* right, and "looks right" isn't the same as "is allowed."

The only rules an AI can't ignore are the ones that **run as code** between its
request and the screen. That's the shift SINA makes: the rules aren't a document
the AI consults, they're a gate it passes through. It can propose anything; only
what's permitted renders.

<Callout variant="governance" title="The short version">
  A design system an AI can *read* is a suggestion. A design system an AI must
  *pass through* is a guarantee.
</Callout>

## Why this is safer than smarter prompts

You could try to make the AI behave with a very careful prompt. But a prompt is
advice, and advice can be misread, forgotten, or overridden by a clever input.
An enforced rule can't be. The AI's output is only ever as safe as what it's
*allowed* to do — so SINA constrains the "allowed," not the "asked."

That's why SINA validates on the server, not in the prompt and not in the browser.
The model can be wrong, jailbroken, or adversarial, and the guarantee still holds.
