---
title: Escalation & enforcement
description: Not every problem is a hard block. SINA has three severities — flag, escalate, reject — each with a different consequence.
---

When the gate finds a problem, it doesn't always slam the door. Each violation
carries a **severity** that decides what happens next. There are three.

<EnforcementLadder />

- **`flag`** is informational. It's recorded on the decision — useful for
  compliance and reporting — but blocks nothing. A $5,000 transfer is flagged
  for review and still goes through.
- **`escalate`** blocks the standard render and **forces a stricter component
  instead**. The over-limit wire escalates to a `SecureWireDialog` that demands
  a second approver. The request isn't refused — it's redirected to a safer
  path.
- **`reject`** is a hard block. Nothing mounts. A fabricated button, smuggled
  card data, or a self-approved transfer is simply refused.

<Callout variant="note" title="A decision can carry several">
  One intent can trip multiple violations at once. The unapproved $60,000 wire
  flags twice (reportable, review) *and* escalates once. The flags are
  recorded; the escalate is what forces the dialog.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="tip" title="Honest note">
  Severity is a property of each rule's logic, not a global on/off switch. To
  "loosen" enforcement you change the rule (or which intents you route through
  the gate) — deliberately, in code, where it's reviewable.
</Callout>

You don't have to govern your whole app on day one — rules are independent, so
you can start with a single intent and expand from there. See [Adopt
incrementally](/docs/guides/adopt-incrementally).
