---
title: Intent & the Constitution
description: Two words do the heavy lifting in SINA. An intent is what the AI asks for; the Constitution is the set of rules that decides whether it's allowed.
---

Two terms come up on every page. Here they are, in one sentence each.

## Intent

An **intent** is the AI's request to show something — a verb plus some data,
never a component.

```ts
const intent = {
  intent: "wire_transfer",
  props: { amount: 6000000, currency: "USD", recipient: /* … */ },
};
```

Notice what's *not* there: no button, no dialog, no layout. The AI describes
*what* it wants ("move this money"), not *how* it should look. Choosing the "how"
is SINA's job — and that's the whole safety trick.

## The Constitution

The **Constitution** is the set of rules that decides what's allowed. Each rule is
a **schema** — a precise, machine-checked description of a valid request. Together
they answer one question for every intent: *is this permitted, and if so, what
should render?*

<Callout variant="note" title="A useful picture">
  Think of the Constitution as the bouncer at the door. The AI can ask for
  anything. Nothing gets on stage until the bouncer checks the request against the
  list and says yes.
</Callout>

## How they fit together

The AI emits an intent. The Constitution checks it. The result decides what
mounts. That's the entire loop — and because the AI only ever sends the intent,
it can't reach past the bouncer.

Every rule in the Constitution is written down and **cited to a real standard** —
a regulation or an explicit policy — so a block is never arbitrary. More on that
in [The standards behind the Constitution](/docs/governance/standards).
